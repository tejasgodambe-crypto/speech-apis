Banking, Financial Services and Insurance (BFSI)Common across multiple use-cases

Industry-specific input scope. Telephone/video-call audio and applicant devices; financial names and quantities; caller/agent prompt echo and hold music.

Language and speaker coverage — all rows. Test each offered language with regional/dialect and Indian-English variation where applicable, monolingual speech, between-turn and within-sentence code-switching, short language-ambiguous replies and realistic accents, ages, voice ranges and speaking styles. Include elderly, child or atypical speech when present in the intended population. Check word/character errors under an explicit mixed-script and spelling-variant policy; do not normalize away wrong names, numbers or negation.

Audio and capture coverage — actual deployment variants. Test clean reference audio alongside the microphones, distance, compression, loudness, clipping, echo/reverberation and background noise that the workflow encounters. For telephone branches include real narrowband 8 kHz and other deployed call formats, crosstalk and received audio affected by loss/dropouts. For microphone branches include actual devices and any noise/echo front end. Preserve recoverable words; missing audio cannot be reconstructed reliably.

Non-speech, competing speech and speaking style. Test silence, long pauses, music/noise-only spans, breathing/coughing/laughter, quiet/fast/emotional speech, hesitations and overlapping or background voices. Expect no invented lexical speech from non-speech. Define whether the output covers all audible speakers or only an isolated target input. Target-speaker selection, echo cancellation and speech separation are front-end tasks; an ordinary mixed-audio ASR cannot be assumed to know whom the application intends.

Recognition boundaries and continuity. Test one-word answers, short negatives, the longest in-scope utterances, the first/last word near recording boundaries, pauses before a critical word, restarts, and internal chunk joins through a name or number. For live branches test premature/late completion, partial revisions if exposed, and supported reconnect behaviour. For long recordings test omitted/duplicated sections and hallucination after long gaps. Score errors only on audio actually received; assess capture loss separately.

Required output and configuration branches. For every capability retained in the feature cell, test its promised output: participant attribution with overlap/transfers when required; timeline offsets over long gaps when required; policy-required disfluencies; and formatted numbers/punctuation only if part of the chosen output. If language routing or contextual vocabulary is actually deployed, test short/mixed-language routing and false insertion of favoured terms. These tests do not make those implementation choices universally mandatory.

Coverage rule for each use-case. Run the group tests above plus that use-case’s specific tests below. Include representative combinations that could change important words, such as a quiet negation in a noisy call or a code-switched identifier across a chunk boundary. Test shared conditions once on reusable corpora where appropriate, but score each relevant domain and population slice. A list of test families is not a claim that every utterance or interaction has been enumerated.
